# Rome landmarks, phase 2 — the plate overlay

Two pictures, both generated by `tools/scratch/rome-landmarks.mjs --plate`, both drawn **in survey
metres onto the georeferenced Lanciani raster with no projection involved at all**. That is the
point of them: the game's frame compresses depth 2.86× and width 2.26×, so a render of the
battlefield cannot be compared with a plan of Rome by eye. These can, because both sides of the
comparison are in real metres.

Regenerate with:

```
node tools/scratch/rome-landmarks.mjs --plate --e0=-1750 --e1=2450 --n0=-800 --n1=2100 \
  --zoom=0.9 --grid=250 --out=screenshots/rome-landmarks/01-survey-on-lanciani-after.png
node tools/scratch/rome-landmarks.mjs --plate --e0=-900 --e1=100 --n0=200 --n1=900 \
  --zoom=2.0 --grid=100 --out=screenshots/rome-landmarks/02-campus-martius-after.png
```

`reference/` is gitignored local-only material and does not exist in a worktree. Symlink it in
first or the tool exits 2 and says so.

## How to read them

- **Red rectangle** — the monument's **real published plan** at its **real bearing** on its **real
  coordinate**, straight out of `src/city/rome/survey.ts`. If a red box does not sit on its own
  inked plan, the survey is wrong about where the building is. That is the fault to look for
  first, and it is the fault this phase spent most of its time on: fourteen rows moved, five of
  them by more than 100 m.
- **Blue rectangle** — what the game actually draws: the same plan scaled by that row's authored
  `draw`. The gap between red and blue is the compression the frame costs, monument by monument.
  **Where there is no blue box the monument is drawn at full published plan** — nine of the
  twenty-seven on the map are.
- **Green** — landscape (`soft`): the Janiculum, the Horti Sallustiani, the Tiber Island. Not
  masonry, exempt from the overlap rule, and never scaled.
- **Cyan grid** — survey metres east and north of the Temple of Jupiter OM, so anything in the
  picture can be measured rather than judged.

## What to look at

**`01-survey-on-lanciani-after.png`** — the whole city. Every red box should sit on ink. The five
boxes with no blue inside them at the bottom of the frame (the Palatine, the Circus Maximus, the
Aventine, the Baths of Caracalla, the Caelian) are past the +Z edge and are not drawn at all; that
is the agreed cost of `KZ` = 0.35 and they are shown so the cost is visible.

**`02-campus-martius-after.png`** — the fought-in city, at 2×. This is where the corrections are:

- the **Baths of Nero** sit on a symmetrical thermae plan with a tinted natatio. The old row put
  them 180 m south of it, on top of the Baths of Agrippa;
- the **Theatre of Pompey** now contains its own cavea arc. The old row was a 300 × 180 box for the
  whole complex centred on the cavea alone, so most of it covered open ground to the west;
- the **Porticus Pompei** is new — the other half of that complex, between the theatre and the Area
  Sacra, with `PORTICVS POMPEIANAE` inked under it;
- the **Iseum Campense** is a long east–west enclosure instead of a 70 × 34 box, which is the
  fabric gate's worst fidelity failure closed.

## What these pictures cannot tell you

The plate resolves **1.709 m/px**. At the zoom where a monument fills the frame its inked *name* is
illegible, so identification has to come from a labelled plate (Shepherd 1923) or from the shape.
Only the geometry comes from Lanciani. And Lanciani was published 1893–1901: it cannot control the
Ludus Magnus (excavated 1937), the Ara Pacis (1937–8), the Area Sacra (1926–9) or most of the
imperial fora. Where a red box sits on blank paper, check the date before concluding anything.
